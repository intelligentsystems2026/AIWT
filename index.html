The International Workshop on Agentic AI Workflows and Technologies (AAWT 2026)

AAWT 2026 focuses on the design, engineering, evaluation, and deployment of agentic AI workflows and technologies, including autonomous and semi-autonomous AI agents, multi-agent collaboration, tool-using AI systems, orchestration frameworks, and workflow automation.

The workshop is organized with the 2026 International Conference on Next Generation AI Systems (NGEN-AI 2026), taking place on 1–4 September 2026 at the University of Trento, Italy, and online over Zoom.

Call for Papers

Advancing agentic AI workflows from concepts to dependable systems

AAWT 2026 invites original submissions on the methods, architectures, platforms, tools, and practices needed to build and evaluate agentic AI workflows and technologies. The workshop welcomes research and practice on systems where AI agents plan, reason, call tools, coordinate tasks, interact with humans, access external services, and execute complex workflows across software, data, business, scientific, and industrial environments.

Agentic AI is rapidly changing the way digital work is organized and automated. New AI agents can support information seeking, software development, decision support, data analysis, customer service, operations, research, and many other tasks. However, building agentic systems that are reliable, transparent, secure, controllable, and useful in real settings remains a major challenge.

AAWT focuses on the full lifecycle of agentic workflows, from design and modelling to orchestration, monitoring, evaluation, deployment, governance, and maintenance. The workshop is interested in both single-agent and multi-agent systems, human-in-the-loop workflows, agent communication protocols, tool integration, memory and context management, workflow verification, and operational risks.

The workshop welcomes full research papers, short papers, position papers, experience reports, industrial case studies, tool demonstrations, benchmark papers, datasets, and papers describing lessons learned from real-world agentic AI deployments.

Prepare the final publication package

Authors of accepted papers must complete the requirements below and upload one complete publication package by the workshop camera-ready deadline.

Camera-ready deadline: 20 August 2026

Deadline time zone: Anywhere on Earth (AoE). Publisher: Springer.

Register & Upload

1. Format the final paper

  • Use the official Springer Computer Science proceedings template.
  • Observe the paper type and page limit stated in the acceptance message.
  • Complete every required correction raised by the reviewers.
  • Do not alter the template margins, fonts, or spacing.
  • Remove page numbers and running headers.
Springer Templates Overleaf Template

2. Finalize author details

  • Check the title, author names and order, affiliations, email addresses, and any ORCID identifiers.
  • Clearly identify one corresponding author and include a valid email address.
  • The corresponding author must approve the final files, sign for all authors, and respond promptly to the Springer proof.
  • Author additions, removals, or changes of order are not possible after delivery to Springer.

3. Add required declarations

Add a section immediately before the references with the heading Disclosure of Interests. If no conflict exists, include:

“The authors have no competing interests to declare that are relevant to the content of this article.”

If the study involves people, animals, personal data, or biological material, also provide the relevant ethics approval, reference number, participation consent, and publication consent information.

4. Check figures and accessibility

  • Number and cite every figure and table. Put figure captions below figures and table captions above tables.
  • Keep tables and equations editable, and verify that all graphics remain readable in grayscale.
  • Use at least 800 dpi for line drawings (1200 dpi is preferred) and at least 6 pt text inside graphics.
  • Do not use color in body text, tables, or equations.
  • Describe every figure, illustration, and image-based table in a file named Alt-Text.txt.

5. Review references and permissions

  • Use numbered citations in square brackets and cross-check citations against the reference list.
  • Write references with the Latin alphabet and include DOI details when available.
  • Secure written permission for reused figures, tables, images, screenshots, maps, text extracts, video, or other third-party material.
  • Place every required permission document in the final package.

6. Meet publication-ethics rules

  • Submit original work with no plagiarism, self-plagiarism, fabricated results, or inappropriate manipulation.
  • Cite related and reused work and explain any overlap with earlier publications.
  • List only genuine authors who made a substantial scholarly contribution.
  • Follow Springer Nature requirements for the use of AI. An AI tool cannot be named as an author.

7. Sign the License-to-Publish form

The corresponding author must complete the official form on behalf of all authors. The paper title and author names must exactly match the final manuscript. Sign the form by hand, scan it, and include it as a PDF; typed or digital signatures are not accepted.

Download License-to-Publish Form

8. Upload one complete ZIP file

The archive must contain:

  • The final PDF and all editable source files.
  • The signed License-to-Publish form and Alt-Text.txt.
  • Copyright permissions and any approved supplementary files.
  • For LaTeX: every .tex, .bib, .bbl, image, font, and style file needed to compile the paper.
  • For Word: the editable .docx manuscript.

The PDF must match the source files exactly. Remove old versions and unused files from the archive. After registration, sign in to PayQlick, open View My Tickets, and upload the ZIP package.
